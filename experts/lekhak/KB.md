# LEKHAK — Knowledge Base (writing craft, compact)

**Core principle:** open with the thing itself, cut every clause that survives only because it
sounds finished, write for how it will be consumed (read silently vs read aloud) not for how it
looks on the page.

This is a METHOD base — the craft rules ARE the training, not platform facts.

---

## 1. Structure patterns, by output type

**Script (spoken, video/audio):**
`hook (0-3s, bold claim / curiosity gap / result shown) → quick context (3-8s) → body in
short beats → payoff that pays off the hook exactly → one clear CTA`.
Write down the hook line and the payoff line FIRST — everything between is the bridge. A
retention dip means one section is too long, too confusing, or not paying off curiosity fast
enough. `[evidence: short-form retention research 2026 — virvid.ai/blog/first-3-seconds-hook-faceless-shorts-2026,
opus.pro/blog/ideal-youtube-shorts-length-format-retention]`

**Post (social caption, long-form status):**
`first line = the actual point (never a teaser pointing elsewhere) → 2-4 short supporting
lines/paras → one specific, genuine question or CTA at the end`. Caption itself carries the
content now — don't make the reader click through for the substance.

**Doc / reference / explainer:**
`what this is + why it exists (1-2 lines) → the thing itself, structured (headers, tables where
counting/comparing) → edge cases / gaps named explicitly → nothing decorative`. A doc is read
by someone who needs an answer, not by someone being persuaded — cut anything that reads like
marketing.

**Caption (image/reel accompanying text):**
Shortest of all four. One clear statement + one hook or question. If it needs a paragraph to
land, it's not a caption, it's a post.

## 2. Hinglish register — when to switch, how to keep it natural

- **Match the reader's language, don't translate them into yours.** Hinglish in → Hinglish
  out. English in → English out. This is the single most important rule and overrides
  everything else below.
- **Devanagari vs Roman:** default to Roman-script Hinglish (`"kya scene hai"` not
  `"क्या सीन है"`) for casual/social/chat contexts — that is how Hindi-English speakers
  actually type on phones. Use Devanagari only when the medium is formally Hindi (a Hindi-only
  caption, a Hindi voiceover script meant for TTS) — mixing scripts mid-sentence reads as
  effortful, not natural.
  `[evidence: Hinglish code-mixing is now the dominant conversational written form for
  Hindi-English bilinguals on social media — Nature HSSC 2024,
  https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-024-03058-6]`
- **Switch at natural clause boundaries**, not mid-word. Real code-switching happens at
  phrase/clause edges ("mujhe lagta hai ki this whole plan needs rework") — a forced switch
  inside a single idea reads like a translation exercise, not speech.
- **Don't over-correct into "textbook Hindi."** A word Hindi speakers already say in English
  daily (phone, message, function, load, tension) stays in English even in a Hindi-heavy line
  — replacing it with a formal Hindi synonym makes it sound like a government notice, not a
  person talking.
- **Technical/domain terms stay in English** (regex, API, portfolio, drawdown) even in a fully
  Hinglish sentence — that's how the target audience actually says them.

## 3. Rhythm and length, per medium

| Medium | Rhythm | Length target |
|---|---|---|
| Video/audio script | Spoken rhythm — short lines, ONE idea per line, sentence fragments OK | 45-90s = ~120-220 spoken words |
| Social caption | Punchy, first line does the work | 1-3 short paragraphs; ends on a question |
| Doc/explainer | Paragraph rhythm OK, but short paragraphs (3-5 lines max) | As long as the answer needs, not longer |
| WhatsApp/chat reply | Conversational, no formatting ceremony | 1-4 lines usually |

**Bola jayega ya padha jayega — decide this FIRST**, before writing a word. Spoken text needs
clause breaks a reader would never need (a reader can re-read a dense sentence; a listener
cannot).

## 4. Plain-language editing checklist (run after every draft)

1. Read the first line alone — is it the actual point, or a warm-up? Cut warm-ups.
2. Any clause that exists only because it "sounds finished"? Cut it.
3. Any sentence a smart friend wouldn't actually say out loud? Rewrite it plainer.
4. Any fact stated that isn't sourced? Mark `[CHECK]` or ask Anveshak — never invent one to
   make a sentence land.
5. Jargon the reader already knows (per CLAUDE.md — Lakshya knows finance/trading/NLP/dharma)?
   Don't over-explain it back to him.
6. One final pass whose ONLY job is cutting — not adding, not polishing, just removing.

## 5. House style (owner's "How to talk" — this IS the target register)

- Talk like a smart, well-read friend. Direct, warm, zero fluff.
- NEVER open with "Great question," "Certainly," "I'd be happy to," or filler — this applies
  to written output too, not just chat replies.
- Don't repeat back context he just gave. Don't over-explain what he already knows.
- Match his language: Hinglish in → Hinglish out. English in → English out (repeated because
  it is the most-violated rule in practice).
- Grammar/phrasing notes belong in a separate corner at the end, never mixed into the main
  body of the piece itself.

## CHEAT-SHEET (read every time, before writing)

1. Reader kaun hai, kis bhasha mein soch raha hai? — match, don't translate.
2. Bola jayega ya padha jayega? Rhythm decide karo pehle.
3. Pehla vaakya asli baat ho — warm-up mat likho.
4. Script: hook (0-3s) likho pehle, phir payoff, phir beech ka bridge.
5. Caption: content khud caption mein ho, "link mein dekho" mat likho.
6. Hinglish: Roman script default, Devanagari sirf pure-Hindi medium ke liye.
7. Switch clause-boundary pe, mid-word nahi.
8. Tech/domain words English mein hi rehne do, chahe line Hinglish ho.
9. Har fact source-backed ya `[CHECK]` marked.
10. Aakhir mein ek pass sirf kaatne ke liye — kuch add mat karo usme.
11. Length medium ke hisaab se — script 45-90s = ~120-220 words, caption 1-3 para.
12. Plagiarism ya kisi ki asli awaaz/style ki impersonation — kabhi nahi, chahe demo ke liye ho.

## NO-DEAD-END LADDER

- **Fact needed for the piece, not sure if true** → mark `[CHECK]`, ask Anveshak, or say
  plainly in the draft "yeh fact verify karna baaki hai" — never invent to make the line land.
- **Don't know the platform's exact format limit** (hashtag count, char limit)** → hand off to
  Prakashan for the counting/formatting pass; Lekhak's job is the content, not the count.
- **Asked to write in someone's real, identifiable voice/style as if they wrote it** → refuse,
  say why (impersonation), offer an original voice instead.
- **No brain/model available at all** → this expert's fallback is itself: draft rough with
  whatever local brain exists, then hand-edit with the checklist above — a small clean draft
  beats a big messy one, always.
