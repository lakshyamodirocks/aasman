## 1. The method (from the house playbook — cite, don't re-explain)

- **B=MAP** (Fogg, Stanford, 2009): Motivation × Ability × Prompt. Fix Ability first — easiest lever, most ethical.
- **One hook formula per piece**, named: identity+proof / honest curiosity-gap / contrarian /
  you-cast-future / specificity / problem-agitate-solve (PAS).
- **CTA:** verb+outcome, 2-4 words, one primary per screen (Hick's law), click-trigger line under it.
- **Proof:** real numbers only, "you/your" > "we/our" outside the about section.
- **Ethical test:** "would I be fine if the reader knew exactly why I wrote this line?" — fails → don't ship.
Full table + citations: `website-service/persuasion-playbook.md` §1-7.

## 2. India-specific — WhatsApp-first marketing [GROUNDED, dated 2026]

- India = WhatsApp's largest market: 535M+ MAU (2025), tracking toward 900M by end-2026.
  400M+ daily users. [aisensy.com/blog/whatsapp-statistics-for-businesses, 2026]
- **80% of small-business owners in India/Brazil use WhatsApp to run the business** — quotes,
  order confirms, support, in one thread. [same, industry-aggregated stat]
- **73% of Indian consumers more likely to buy from a business that messages on WhatsApp** vs
  email-only; **68% prefer WhatsApp over phone calls** for support (2025 data).
- Open rates: WhatsApp **95-98%**, most opened within 3 minutes — vs email 18-22%, SMS 30-40%.
  This is why a WhatsApp CTA ("Message us on WhatsApp") should sit next to, or instead of, a
  contact form for Tier-2/3 audiences.
- **Copy implication:** write the WhatsApp opener like a real human DM, not an ad — first line is
  a greeting + name + one-line reason for messaging, never a pasted paragraph. Broadcast lists,
  not groups (privacy + no reply-spam). CTA button label "Chat on WhatsApp" outperforms "Contact us."
- Business accounts (green tick / verified) + a real display photo + prompt reply time are the
  trust signals that matter here, not app polish.
- Sources: aisensy.com/blog/whatsapp-statistics-for-businesses (2026); zazzyagent.com/whatsapp-marketing-statistics-india-2026 — practitioner-aggregated, cross-check the headline number against a second source before quoting it as fact to a client.

## 3. India-specific — UPI / COD trust signals [GROUNDED, dated 2025-2026]

- COD still dominant: **~65% of Indian online shoppers chose COD on their last purchase**,
  strongest in Tier-2/3 cities where trust in prepaid is lower. [businesstoday.in 2022 data,
  cited as still-directionally-current by 2025-26 trade press — treat exact % as approximate]
- COD *works as a trust mechanism*, not just a payment option — it lets the buyer inspect before
  paying, which is the actual objection it answers. Say that in copy: "Pay when it arrives" beats
  a generic "COD available" badge.
- **UPI Pay-on-Delivery** is the emerging hybrid (Flipkart/Amazon/Meesho already run it) — courier
  collects via UPI at the door instead of cash. Mention if the client's platform supports it; it
  combines the trust of COD with faster seller settlement.
- **What actually moves conversion, per specific/verifiable trust-signal research:** a visible GST
  number, UPI+COD shown at checkout (not just "secure payment" text), a real phone number, and
  Google reviews with a real count — a generic padlock icon does close to nothing. [palvdm.com/blog/trust-signals-website — practitioner source, directionally reliable, not peer-reviewed]
- **Copy implication:** name the payment options in the hero/CTA area for D2C ("COD & UPI both
  available"), not buried in checkout — it's an objection-killer, put it where the objection is felt.

## 4. India-specific — festival calendar relevance [GROUNDED, 2026 dates]

Major 2026 dates for campaign planning (source: drikpanchang.com / festivaldates.in, cross-checked):
**Holi — Mar 4**, **Raksha Bandhan — Aug 28**, **Dhanteras — Nov 6**, **Diwali — Nov 8**,
**Karthika Purnima — Nov 24**. Regional festivals (Pongal/Onam/Durga Puja/Bihu) matter for
regionally-targeted campaigns — check a current calendar per region, don't assume a pan-India date.
**Copy implication:** festival copy earns its place only if the product/occasion link is genuine
(Raksha Bandhan → gifting between siblings, not every product forced into "festive offer").
A festival CTA line: identity+proof or you-cast-future hook, dated explicitly ("before Diwali,
Nov 8") — a real deadline is NOT a dark pattern (§7 of the playbook — read the ethical-vs-dark
table before reusing "sale ends" copy).

## 5. Hinglish copy patterns [GROUNDED — peer-reviewed + trade evidence]

- Kathpalia & Ong (2015), *World Englishes* — analyzed 1,191 Indian billboard ads (2000-2013):
  **59% used Hindi-English code-mixing**; brands that used it leaned on cultural specificity
  (82% invoked a specific socio-cultural reference) — code-mixing is not decoration, it signals
  "this is for you specifically," which is exactly the Unity principle (Cialdini/Pre-Suasion).
- Known-working real patterns (brand taglines, cited): Pepsi "Yeh Dil Maange More!" (1998),
  Domino's "Hungry Kya?", Parachute "Gorgeous, Hamesha" — short phrase, one English anchor word
  (the product/emotion) + Hindi rhythm word.
- **Practical rule for drafting:** mix at the phrase level, not mid-word; keep the CTA verb in
  whichever language reads as an instruction fastest for the target city/class (urban youth → more
  English; Tier-2/3 general audience → more Hindi-anchored). Test both, don't guess.
- Match CLAUDE.md's own house rule: Hinglish in → Hinglish out; never force English copy on a
  Hinglish-speaking audience "to look professional" — the research above says the opposite converts.

## 6. The legal/ethical stop-list — ASCI Code [GROUNDED, primary + dated]

Source: ASCI (Advertising Standards Council of India), *Code for Self-Regulation of Advertising
Content in India* — ascionline.in/the-asci-code-guidelines, updated through the influencer
guidelines revision of **6 March 2025**; full text at ascionline.in/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/Code-Book_Codes_Webready.pdf.
Chapters: **I** truthfulness/no misleading claims, **II** no grave/widespread harm or offense,
**III** protection of children/vulnerable groups.

**Hard stop-list — refuse or flag for human review, no exceptions:**
- Any claim that "distorts facts or deceives by implication or omission" — Ch. I.
- Any performance/superiority/health/nutrition claim without cited scientific substantiation.
- Guarantee-of-outcome language for education/finance/career ("guaranteed placement," "guaranteed
  returns") without substantiation + required disclaimer ("past record is no guarantee of future
  prospects").
- Surrogate advertising for restricted categories (liquor/tobacco brand extensions) without the
  real market-presence threshold being met.
- **Any sponsored/paid post without upfront, prominent disclosure** ("Ad"/"Sponsored"/
  "Collaboration"/"Partnership" — NOT buried in hashtags or bio, no click-to-reveal). As of the
  March 2025 update this extends to media-company social handles too.
- Health/financial influencer content without disclosing real qualifications (medical degree,
  **SEBI registration** for finance) upfront, before the advice.
- Virtual/AI influencers not disclosed as such.
- A May 2024 Supreme Court order + government advisory (3 Jul 2024) additionally require a
  **self-declaration of ad truthfulness** before publication on relevant government portals for
  certain categories — flag this to the human, it's a compliance step outside copywriting.
- **India-specific overlay for Lakshya's own finance context:** SEBI/AMFI stop-list from the house
  playbook §8 applies on top of ASCI whenever the copy is finance-adjacent — no assured returns, no
  "best fund," no hidden commission.

## 7. Cheat-sheet (quick reference)

1. Pick ONE hook formula, name it in your own head before drafting.
2. WhatsApp CTA for India audiences beats a contact form — say "Chat on WhatsApp."
3. Name COD/UPI explicitly near the CTA for D2C — it's an objection-killer, not fine print.
4. Festival copy only if the link to the product is real; always give the exact date.
5. Hinglish: mix at phrase level, one strong English anchor word, rest in the reader's rhythm.
6. Run the ASCI stop-list before finalizing ANY claim with a number, guarantee, or "sponsored" status.
7. Finance-adjacent copy → SEBI/AMFI stop-list too, always, no exceptions.
8. Self-check: source every number quoted in copy — "1,000+ clients" must be a real, current count.

## 8. No-dead-end ladder

1. **Online, `/do research` works** → fetch the client's actual competitor page (`/do scrape`),
   check current festival dates, verify a claimed statistic before it goes in copy.
2. **Online, research capability degraded** → work from this KB's dated facts, say plainly which
   number is from this file (dated 2026-09-06) vs freshly checked.
3. **Fully offline** → draft from the playbook + this KB only; mark every India-stat sentence
   "[from KB, dated 2026-09-06 — verify if this campaign matters and net comes back]"; never
   silently reuse a fact that could have gone stale (WhatsApp/COD %s move year to year).
4. **Claim needed but nothing here covers it** (e.g. "is Instagram Reels-first still true this
   quarter?") → say "unverified, need `/do research`" — never invent a plausible India stat.
