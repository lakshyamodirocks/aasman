# Why Aasmaan exists

I kept watching the same thing happen. A free AI tier would show up, people would build a habit around it, and months later it would shrink, get paywalled, or die outright — a free CLI login killed in an afternoon, a project relicensed within its first week, free tiers cut by half overnight. Every time, the people who lost the most were the ones who could least afford a subscription to replace it.

So the question I actually sat with was: if an AI researcher from ten years in the future looked at today's phones — not flagship phones, the affordable kind, on a tight data plan, in Hindi, held by someone who has never typed a word to an AI in their life — what would they ship for free, today, that keeps working no matter which company changes its mind next month?

That's this. Not a chatbot wrapper, not "ChatGPT but cheaper." A program that adapts to your phone — or your laptop, or the two paired together — instead of asking your device to adapt to it: a smaller brain on a smaller device, the same respect either way. It stays local when it can and reaches a free-tier cloud only when you hand it a key you own. It never asks to hold your login for you.

Trust isn't a feature I added at the end. It's the actual product. The single fear that came up over and over, in one form or another, was: "they'll steal my key." So every network call sits in one file you can read. Nothing phones home. And the claim that this thing doesn't talk behind your back isn't something you have to take on faith — put the phone in airplane mode and watch it keep answering.

I'm building this from Jaipur, on my own, thinking across finance, behavioural coaching, and software the way I've always worked — which is probably why the finance-flavoured expert is built to explain rather than advise, and why nothing here is designed to hold your attention longer than it needs to. It isn't built on top of any one tradition, and it isn't neutral by accident: it is meant to work the same way for whoever picks it up, whatever they believe, in whatever language they think in.

What I won't do: chase whichever model is newest this week for its own sake, force a cloud account on anyone, add anything that measures your attention instead of your outcome, or pretend this is a competitor to ChatGPT — a chat box is a stage most people will outgrow, not the destination.

"Beta" here doesn't mean broken and left alone. It means worked on, out loud, in the open. Feedback is read at least weekly; the day it lands it gets tagged by how urgent it is, and anything at the top of that ranking gets looked at immediately, not queued behind a roadmap. If something in this document reads stale by the time you read it — a model got discontinued, a number moved — that's expected, not a broken promise. Say so, and it gets fixed. That loop is the actual reason this can be trusted to stay useful, not just useful on day one.

— Lakshya, Jaipur
